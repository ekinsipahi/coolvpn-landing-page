Why most free Android VPN apps are a bad trade

Search the Play Store for a free VPN for Android and you'll get hundreds of results; ask the internet which is the best free VPN for Android and you'll get a hundred confident answers, most of them affiliate pages. Running a VPN network costs real money every month, so each one of those apps is funded somehow — and if the listing won't tell you how, the answer is usually you.

The three patterns worth recognising: a data cap small enough that the app is really a trial wearing a free label; a timer that turns the thing off after a week; and the genuinely dangerous one, an app that sells the browsing data it collects. A VPN sees all of your traffic by design, which makes it the single worst app on your phone to hand that business model to.

We're the first kind of honest and not the second: VPNsterr's free Android tier is funded by ads inside the app, and the Play listing says Contains ads because that's true. There's no data cap, no countdown, and nothing is logged — we can't sell browsing history we never recorded.

What "no logs" means here, specifically

Plenty of mobile VPN apps promise no logs and then keep connection records "for troubleshooting". Ours is a structural claim instead of a promise: the system never creates an activity log in the first place, so there is nothing to leak, subpoena or quietly change our minds about later.

What we do keep is the boring part any company taking payments has to keep: your account, and a billing record if you ever subscribed. Those are spelled out in the privacy policy, and you can wipe the account yourself from inside the app.

The same architecture runs behind the Android app, the browser extension and the desktop clients — it isn't a separate, weaker promise for mobile.
